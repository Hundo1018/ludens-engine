# 17.86 — strand torsion integration

Question (2026-10-27): can `StrandSet` / `Rope` and `CosseratRod` share one torsion model, so a
strand can twist, store that twist, and hand it to a fluid or contact coupling?

## Finding: the gap is in the state, not in the solver

| type | state per particle / segment | twist DOF | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CosseratRod` | position `x`, segment quaternion `q`, angular velocity `omega` | yes: `twist_angle(j)` from the Darboux vector | `physics/cosserat_rod.mojo:52-63`, `:137-145` |
| `StrandSet` | position `x`, velocity `v`; direction projection, no frame | no | `physics/strand.mojo:69-87`; `:41-43` states "no twist" in the model |
| `Rope` | position `x`, `prev`, `v`; XPBD distance constraints | no | `physics/rope.mojo:32-47` |

So "integrating" `StrandSet` with `CosseratRod` cannot be a call that passes twist across: a `StrandSet`
strand has no frame to carry a twist angle. Building a `CosseratRod` from a strand's particles gives
frames from the chords, and a planar strand's twist is zero by construction.

## Decision

Add one optional per-particle state to `StrandSet`: a twist angle `twist[i]` (rad, about the chord
carried by the segment ending at particle i), default 0, with a relaxation rate `torsion` (1/s, default
0 = off).

Scope, narrowed after the advisor's review: `StrandSet` has no material frames, so a twist cannot be a
frame constraint as `CosseratRod._bend_twist` is. A scalar twist moves no position on its own. It is
observable in one place only: the shaped bending target (`_step_shaped`), which the twist rotates about
the previous segment's direction. A straight strand has target == prev_dir, so twist has no positional
effect there. Item (1) is therefore scoped to curled strands. This is the honest outcome: a straight
strand does not twist in this model, and nothing here pretends it does.

Serialization: `strands_write` / `strands_read` carry `twist` per particle (format change for the strand
stream; round-trip tested by `test_strand_ext`).

Rejected alternative: make `Rope` a `CosseratRod` wrapper. That would change `Rope`'s public
behaviour (tie, attach, quarantine) and its serialization in one step. Keep the three types and add
the adapter only where a coupling needs twist.

## Interface (as implemented)

- `StrandSet.twist: List[Real]` per particle (default 0); `StrandSet.torsion: Real` (1/s, default 0).
  The constructor takes `torsion` as its last argument, so existing calls are unchanged.
- `StrandSet.set_root_twist(s, phi)`: sets the root twist of strand `s`.
- `_step_shaped`: `target` turned about `prev_dir` by `twist[i]` when `twist[i] != 0`; then
  root-to-tip relaxation `twist[i] += (twist[i-1] - twist[i]) * min(torsion dt, 1)` when `torsion > 0`.
- Not done: `rod_from_strand` (copying a strand into a `CosseratRod`). The earlier planned
  parity against `CosseratRod` on the same configuration is not implemented; the test uses an analytic
  prediction instead (below).

## Test (`tests/test_strand_torsion.mojo`, integration, 6 checks)

1. One kink (3 particles, segment 2 turned 0.5 rad about z), twist 0.7 on the joint: the tip after one
   step equals x1 + l(cos a, sin a cos φ, sin a sin φ). Analytic, not a second solver. The first run
   failed at the 1e-9 m bound with max error 3.7e-9 m (f32 `Real`); the bound was then set to 1e-7 m.
   This is recorded as a post-run change.
2. The same kink with no twist is planar (same bound).
3. Stiff torsion copies the root twist to every joint in one step.
4. With torsion off, joints keep twist 0 after 1 s.
5. A 12-particle curl with root twist 0.7 and stiff torsion leaves the xy-plane (|z| > 1 mm); without
   twist it stays in plane (|z| < 1e-6 m).

Item (2) of the roadmap entry (plectoneme measurement) is already done on `CosseratRod` alone
(`tests/test_rod_plectoneme.mojo`, `tests/test_rod_greenhill.mojo`); its constants are recorded as
unvalidated against the analytic form.

## Parity with `CosseratRod` (`tests/test_rod_strand_twist.mojo`, component, 3 checks)

The two models have no shared twist quantity: `CosseratRod.twist_angle` is a Darboux component,
`StrandSet.twist` is a rest-target rotation. The comparison is on equilibrium tip positions.

| case | result | prediction |
|---|---|---|
| one kink, rod: segment 0 clamped and turned by φ about its d3 | tip − analytic 8.1e-9 m | within 1e-5 m |
| 12-point curl, rod: segment 0 turned by φ | tip − rigid rotation of rest tip 2.5e-7 m | within 2e-3 m |
| 12-point curl, StrandSet: stiff torsion, root twist φ | tip − rigid rotation 0.142 m | > 1 cm |

Finding: for one joint the two models agree (both reproduce the analytic tip). For a chain they
do not. The rod with a twist-free rest darboux carries the rest shape rigidly about segment 0's
axis, because each joint's rest frame is conjugated along with segment 0. `StrandSet` applies the
twist to each joint's own bend plane, so the twists accumulate and the curl becomes a helix.
Therefore `rod_from_strand` would be lossy for twisted chains. The integration is closed as a
one-joint parity plus this stated non-equivalence, not as a chain-level equivalence.

## Coupling (item (3)) with the existing couplings (`tests/test_strand_contact_twist.mojo`, component, 4/4)

The air coupling is `StrandAir` (17.61) and the contact coupling is `StrandSet`'s sphere and
capsule push-out, plus `strands_collide` for rigid scenes. Both act on particle positions and
velocities, so a twisted curl reaches them without change. Measured: a 12-particle curl against a
sphere (radius 3 cm, centre (0.05, 0.02, 0)), with and without root twist 0.7. Contact is active
in both runs (clearance 0.0 at the surface), and the twisted tip ends 0.20 m from the untwisted one
with |z| 0.137 m versus 0. The first run had the sphere off the strand and so did not test contact;
the sphere was moved and an engagement check added.

Rigid-scene contact (`tests/test_strand_scene_twist.mojo`, integration, 4/4): the same curl, pushed by
`strands_collide` against a static box (half extents (0.03, 0.03, 0.1), centre (0.05, 0.02, 0)), with
μ = 0.3. Engagement is checked first: 1965 contacts in the twisted run, 1079 in the untwisted one. No
particle centre ends inside the box. The twisted tip ends 0.185 m from the untwisted one, with |z| 0.140 m
against 0.

Air (`tests/test_strand_air_twist.mojo`, integration, 5/5): the same curl in `StrandAir` (two-way,
24^3 lattice, 0.4 m/s wind, 600 steps). The shaped bending target sets the outcome. At bend 1.0 and
0.2 the target cancels the wind's displacement of the untwisted curl (x shift −7e-9 m and −2.5 mm). At
bend 0 the wind moves the curl 0.167 m but twist does nothing (no target). The run uses bend 0.05:
untwisted x shift 9 mm, twisted 0.107 m with |z| 0.138 m against 0; air impulse about 2.9e-3 N s;
momentum error 4.3e-4 relative; the two impulses cancel exactly. This bend change was made after a
failed first run; the sweep is recorded in the test header.

## Status

Item (1), twisted curled strands in `StrandSet` with an analytic test, is implemented. Its parity
with `CosseratRod` is closed as above (one-joint agreement, chain non-equivalence recorded).
Item (3), the three couplings (sphere, box via `strands_collide`, air via `StrandAir`), is measured
with twist. The measurements do not show that a twisted frame must reach the contact normal: the
contacts act on particle positions, and the twist enters through the shape target alone.

## Design: a frame-reading contact or air model (not implemented)

Written 2026-10-27. The measured couplings act on particle positions, so they do not read the frame.
A model that does read it is needed only when a force depends on the strand's local axes. Two cases:

1. Anisotropic drag. `StrandAir` applies one drag rate to every particle, so a twisted strand and an
   untwisted one feel the same drag for the same velocity. A ribbon, or a hair with a flat section,
   has a normal drag `d_n` and a tangential drag `d_t` in its own frame. The projected drag then
   changes with the twist angle.
2. Frame-normal contact. A flat section makes a contact normal depend on the frame, not on the
   particle positions alone.

Concrete check, to run before any code is written: a straight ribbon of 40 particles in the
`StrandAir` setup of `tests/test_strand_air_twist.mojo`, held at twist 0 and at twist pi / 2, with
wind along x. Clause 1 (expected from inertness, not a test of the frame): with the current
isotropic drag, the tip displacement and the x impulse are equal for the two twists, because twist
enters only through the shape target and the target is inert at bend 0. Clause 2 is the
discriminator: under a frame model with `d_n` = 4 `d_t`, the two twists differ by more than 10 % in
tip displacement. Clause 2 is what would show that the model needs to read the frame.

Consumer: 17.57 (hair, muscle). It is the first item that needs anisotropic drag. Until 17.57 asks
for it, the frame-reading model is not built, and the closure of 17.86 (3) rests on the three
measured couplings above.

Status: design written and the check is specified; the check has not been run.
