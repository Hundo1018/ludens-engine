# Rendering tier 2: terrain, decals, trails (#140)

Tier 2 of `docs/capability-gaps.md` ("Rendering and audio beyond the top ten"). Three passes, each a `RenderPass`
(`app/render_pass.mojo`) with its CPU side in `game/`:

| feature | CPU side | pass | tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| terrain | `game/terrain.mojo` | `app/terrain_pass.mojo` | `test-tier2` (`tests/terrain.mojo`), `test-tier2-native` (`tests/terrain_native.mojo`) |
| decals | `game/decals.mojo` | `app/decal_pass.mojo` | `test-tier2` (`tests/decals_trails.mojo`), `test-tier2-native` (`tests/tier2_native.mojo`) |
| trails / ribbons | `game/trails.mojo` | `app/trail_pass.mojo` | same as decals |

Native against wasm64 (CPU side, equal bytes): `pixi run -e wasm tier2-same`. Native against the browser (pixels):
the tier 2 row of `?selftest=parity` (`app/tier2_frame.mojo`, below).

```mojo
comptime Passes = Then[DefaultPasses, Then[TerrainPass, Then[DecalPass, TrailPass]]]
var r = SceneRendererOf[Passes](device, format, view)
r.passes.second.first.set_field(device, TerrainField.from_heightfield(collider_heights))
```

Order matters: the terrain is opaque and writes depth; decals and trails test depth and do not write it, so they come
after every opaque pass.

## Terrain

### Data: the collider's heights

`TerrainField` is ludens-core's `collision.HeightField` layout: `h[iz * nx + ix]` at world (ox + ix cell, oz + iz cell).
`TerrainField.from_heightfield(hf)` copies a collider's heights, origin and cell. At level 0 every chunk's triangles are
`HeightField.tri(t)`, corner for corner and in the same order, with the same alternating diagonal ((ix + iz) & 1), so
what is drawn at level 0 is what is hit. `tests/terrain.mojo` compares all 24 576 vertices of a 64 x 64-cell field
generated by core's `procedural.terrain_gen`: 0 differ. After `HeightField.deform`, `update_rows` copies the changed
rows and `TerrainPass.upload_heights` re-uploads only those corners.

### Chunks and level of detail

The grid is cut into chunks of `chunk` cells a side (a power of two; default 64, levels 0 .. 6). At level l a chunk is
a grid of `chunk >> l` cells of 2^l corners each, so its corners are a subset of the full grid and its heights are the
field's own (no resampling). The level is picked from the distance d between the eye and the chunk's box (its x, z
extent and its height range): 0 while d < `lod_distance`, then one more each time d doubles. `prepare` drops chunks
whose bounding sphere is outside the view (`app/cull.mojo` `capsule_outside`), groups the rest by level and uploads one
`vec4<u32>` per chunk; `record_color` draws one instanced draw per level present (at most 7). The vertex shader builds
each vertex from `vertex_index` and the chunk record (`TerrainField.chunk_vertex` is the same arithmetic, and the
CPU tests run it).

### No cracks between levels

On an edge shared with a coarser neighbour (level L > l), each vertex of the finer chunk whose index along the edge is
not a multiple of 2^L moves onto the coarse corner below it. The finer chunk then has exactly the coarser chunk's edge
vertices (same corner index, same height, bit for bit), the triangles between them collapse to zero area, and the
rasteriser's shared-edge rule covers every pixel. This works for any difference of levels, so the levels need no
balancing.

Measured (lavapipe, 480 x 270, a camera above a 64 m field that sees only terrain, chunk levels forced to a mix of
0 .. 4):

| stitching | pixels showing the empty frame |
|---|---:|
| vertices moved onto the coarse corners (built) | 0 |
| vertices placed on the coarse segment (linear height, a T-junction) | 1 |
| none (negative control) | 912 |

The middle row was the first version: the vertex is on the coarse edge only up to float rounding, and that left one
uncovered pixel. On the CPU, `tests/terrain.mojo` checks every shared edge of a 4 x 4-chunk field with forced levels:
24 edges, 21 of them between different levels, 0 whose two sides have different vertices; with stitching off, all 21
differ.

### Splat map and shading

Four 8-bit weights per corner (`TerrainField.splat`, RGBA per corner), the share of four texture array layers
(`TerrainPass.layers`, default all layer 0, the white layer) times four tints (`TerrainPass.tints`). The fragment shader
reads the weights bilinearly at its own (x, z), so the texturing does not change with the level. `splat_by_height_slope`
makes a starting map (layer 0 low, 1 middle, 2 high, 3 steep). Textures are sampled at world (x, z) * `tiling`.

The normal comes from central differences of the grid, bilinear between the four corners of the fragment's cell, so the
shading does not change with the level either. Lighting is the primitive pass's fixed sun (0.4 + 0.6 max(n . sun, 0)).

### Memory

Heights and splat weights are storage buffers of 4 bytes per corner each. A 4097 x 4097 grid is 67 MB + 67 MB, under
WebGPU's default `maxStorageBufferBindingSize` of 128 MiB. A larger grid needs a raised limit or a split into tiles.

## Decals

A decal is a box: centre, unit axes u (width), v (height), w (the direction it is projected along), half extents.
`DecalMesh` clips triangles under it against the box's six planes (Sutherland-Hodgman) and keeps the result as its own
small mesh, with uv from the box's (x, y), a fade = cos of the angle between the surface normal and the projection, and
each vertex lifted 4 mm along the surface normal so it passes the depth test against the surface.

- `project_terrain(field)`: the level-0 triangles of the cells under the box. Far chunks are drawn at a coarser level, so
  `DecalSet.max_distance` (default 60 m) should stay inside the distance where chunks are level 0 or 1, or the decal
  floats above or sinks below the coarser surface.
- `project_mesh(positions, indices, pos, q, half)`: a mesh placed as a `DrawItem` (rotation, per-axis scale, translation,
  as the mesh pass does); triangles whose bounds miss the box are skipped before clipping.
- `project_triangle`: any world-space triangle.

Winding is not trusted; a triangle's normal is taken as the side facing the projector, and a triangle edge-on to w is
skipped. A surface behind the hit one inside the box (the far side of a thin wall) is caught too: keep the box's depth
below the thickness of what is hit.

Per-frame budget (`DecalSet`): at most `max_decals` kept (default 256; adding past it replaces the oldest, counted in
`replaced`), at most `max_triangles` drawn a frame (default 16 384, the pass's buffer), newest first; a decal that does
not fit is not drawn that frame (`over_budget`), one past `max_distance` neither (`far`). Overlapping decals are drawn
oldest first, so a newer one covers an older one. Layer 0 draws a soft disc filling the box; another layer samples the
texture array at the decal's uv. Grazing hits fade: alpha times smoothstep(0.15, 0.4, fade).

Checked on the CPU against worked values: the clipped area on flat ground, a 45 degree slope, flat terrain and a
cube's top face (also rotated) is 2 hu x 2 hv within 1e-6; the fade on the slope is cos 45; an edge-on wall and a
triangle outside the box give nothing; capacity, budget and distance counters. On lavapipe: the scorch darkens the
terrain at its centre from (134, 114, 88) to (37, 30, 24), the mark on the cube is (213, 30, 31) over a grey (71, 73, 76)
face, and a budget of 0 triangles draws the frame without decals byte for byte with both decals counted over budget.

## Trails and ribbons

A trail follows a moving point (`Trail.move_to(time, x, y, z)`): a point is added when the position is at least
`min_step` from the last one, so the spacing does not depend on the frame rate; `expire(time)` drops points older than
`lifetime`; past `max_points` the oldest is dropped (`dropped`). Width and colour follow the point's age
t = age / lifetime: `curve_at(width_curve, t)` and `color3(start, mid, end, t)`, the two functions the particle records
use, so a trail and a particle of the same age and values match. A style is camera-facing (a weapon swing) or `flat`
(width along tangent x up: a tyre mark).

Shared with the particle pass (#121): the camera header, the blend pipelines (`pulled_pipeline`, alpha and additive,
depth tested, not written), alpha segments of all trails sorted far to near along the camera's forward axis with the
particles' `merge_order` (ties by index, so the order is total) and drawn first, additive ones after unsorted, and on the
CPU `curve_at` / `color3`. It is a pass of its own because a particle is one point and a segment is two. The tangent at
a point (previous to next) is shared by the two segments meeting there, so their edges join.

Deterministic: `+ - * /` and `sqrt` on Float64 only. The same calls pack to the same bytes (`tests/decals_trails.mojo`),
on native and on wasm64 (`tier2-same`: equal hashes of the packed records, together with the stitched terrain vertices
and the decal triangles). On lavapipe two crossing alpha trails added in either order give the same frame byte for
byte, and the additive tyre mark raises the pixel under it from (103, 88, 68) to (123, 180, 239).

## Reference images: native and browser

`app/tier2_frame.mojo` draws one fixed frame (480 x 270, RGBA8Unorm): terrain from core's generator with splat by
height and slope, a mesh cube on it, a decal on the terrain and one on the cube, a camera-facing alpha trail and a flat
additive trail. `tests/tier2_native.mojo` checks its 16 x 9 tile means against `tests/golden/tier2_frame_tiles.txt`
(within 2 / 255; `tier2_native --record` rewrites it, `--dump` writes `build/tier2_ref.rgba`), and
`tests/terrain_native.mojo` the terrain alone against `tests/golden/terrain_frame_tiles.txt`. The web app exports the
frame (`app_tier2_request`, `app_tier2_ptr`, `app_tier2_pitch`); `?selftest=parity` compares it with
`build/tier2_native.rgba` (`scripts/parity_pixels_native.py`) under the browser's row of `shell/web/pixel_tolerance.json`,
and requires at least 21 pixels of the cube's red decal and 41 of the blue tyre mark in the native frame so an empty or
terrain-only frame cannot pass. `web-check` and `.github/workflows/parity.yml` run it.

## Not done here

- Terrain: no shadows (neither cast nor received), fog, point lights or atmosphere; no geomorphing, so a chunk changing
  level pops; no holes; no streaming or tiling past one storage buffer (see Memory); the scene format has no terrain
  component yet.
- Decals: no normal or roughness decals (colour only); decals on skinned meshes are not supported (the mesh is taken in
  its bind pose); a coarse terrain chunk under a decal does not match the level-0 triangles it was cut from.
- Trails: no texture along the strip (a solid colour with soft edges); no collision with the world.
- Reference images: the browser row was run in this container's headless Chromium (see the PR); Firefox runs in CI.
